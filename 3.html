What We Need

October 19, 2026

What do we need as a society? We need knowledge. Knowledge is the means by which every conscious organism flourishes. Without knowledge, all conscious life would die out nearly immediately. What do we need to know? What are the most important things people need to understand to improve their lives and--by implication--those of the rest of humanity?

How to Know

For most of human history, people have had no clear idea about how they can know the world. They are unsure of the method by which to acquire knowledge, and so they either deny the possibility or pick some mangled pseudo-epistemology. The metaphysical analogue of these alternatives are subjectivism and intrinsicism. The denial of knowledge generally comes from the belief that the mind is the creator of reality; that truth is dependent on the mind of the person perceiving it. Mystic, rationalist, and other similarly impoverished epistemological stances deny the mind as the identifier of reality. A man, missing a proper method of reasoning, attempts to know reality by magic, floating logic, or some other means which violates the identity of his mind. The proper epistemology is the objective one: the one that includes the mind and reality. The objective approach to epistemology is detailed in books like How We Know and Understanding Objectivism. To grasp objectivity, one must begin with a proper theory of perception.

Direct Realism & Ecological Psychology

Reality is real. Existence exists. It is specifically what it is independently of what people pretend that it is or wish it would be. You cannot wish that an apple were a kilogram of gold. If you wanted to change it into gold, you'd need to perform a complex, adaptive process requiring a great deal of thought and consistent productive action. You must actually perceive reality in order to identify what this process would entail. You'd have to look at the apple, differentiate it from gold, form the concepts required, identify general principles to guide your action, engage in a deductive process to choose between alternatives, etc. Ecological Psychology is the science of direct perception. I believe that the insights of ecological psychologists are revolutionary for the field of psychology. It's something about which I know very little, and that I am eager to learn more about.

Selfish, Objective Ethics

The Objectivist theory of ethics is based on facts of life. Everybody already understands it implicitly. They say, "if your goal is to jump higher, you should do train your ability to jump." "If you want to use a private currency, you should use Monero." Objectivist ethics says, whatever it is that you want to do, there are objective requirements for living as a human. We do not thrive by no means. We do not mindlessly pursue our goals. We must use reason to identify what we must do to flourish or we will fail to achieve anything we want. Maybe the worst cancer pervading the philosophical canon is the ethical doctrine of altruism. Until one grows out of it, he will continue to hate himself for not living up to the impossible standard he's set himself, and he will hate every man that is productive and self-serving.
