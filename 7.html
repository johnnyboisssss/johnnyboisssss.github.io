Propping the Door

November 8, 2026

As I write this, the door of my college dorm room is open. I notice that as I do this, I feel different emotions. Some are vulnerable, some are spiteful. Sometimes I feel fear. The reason I feel this way is because nobody else does this. Everybody's door is always shut. I can see people walking past and looking at me. I wonder if my hair looks okay. I wonder if my face is attractive enough that they aren't thinking I'm a freak. I switch rapidly between thinking, "I'm doing this to spite all of them. I'm doing this because they should have to look at the ugliness I get to impose upon them. I'm doing this because they're too scared to. I want them to judge me. I want them to hate me. I want them to feel disgusted and scared. I want them to be only able to give me the side eye and I want to fight the person who dares to say anything more than that to anyone besides the ones gossiping behind my back," and "I want people to be my friend. I want them to see me relaxed in this room. I want to set a new trend of propping the door so I can greet others as I walk around the halls. I hope they enjoy seeing me. I hope people look at me and smile."

I get to choose whether I give credence to spite or to benevolence. I get to choose whether I want to live my life in a way that achieves values or that embraces suffering, coping, and antipathy. I think the biggest change in my emotional life over the last year has been my decision to embrace benevolence. I recognize more than ever the need for values. I can choose to repress and evade the pain that comes with failing to achieve them, or I can experience it fully, leaning in and accepting the hurt.

When I think about wanting them to hate me, or feeling hatred at their side-eyed glances, that's the emotional mark of a skilled reaction-formation coper. "The grapes are probably sour anyway," the fox proclaimed after kicking the prop out from under the door. The greatest cope is to disguise, or worse, avoid, value-seeking activity by seeing it as something other than it is: the most important, virtuous, and beautiful part of life. I'm choosing to leave my door open not out of spite. I'm doing it because I want people to like me. I'm going to do what I can to befriend people. I know I need to work on my looks to accomplish this. I'll keep on that as well.
